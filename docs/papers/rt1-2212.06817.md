# RT-1 (arXiv 2212.06817): explained

**Title:** RT-1: Robotics Transformer for Real-World Control at Scale
**Authors:** Brohan et al. (Google / Everyday Robots), 2022 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06817) · [project](https://robotics-transformer1.github.io)

## 1. The problem
NLP and vision got general models by training big networks on large, diverse, task-agnostic data. The question here is whether robotics can do the same: can one high-capacity model trained on lots of diverse real robot data generalize to new instructions, objects, and scenes?

## 2. Method
- **Data:** about 130k teleop demos collected over 17 months on 13 mobile manipulators. There are 700+ language instructions over 12 skills (pick, move near, place upright, knock over, open/close drawer, put into a receptacle, and so on).
- **Model (~35M params):** an ImageNet EfficientNet-B3 with **FiLM** conditioning on a sentence embedding of the instruction gives 81 tokens per image. **TokenLearner** compresses that to 8 tokens per image. The history is 6 images, so 48 tokens, which feed an 8-layer decoder transformer.
- **Actions:** 11 dimensions (7 arm, 3 base, 1 mode), each **discretized into 256 bins** and trained with cross-entropy. It runs at 3 Hz.
- It is plain imitation learning, with no RL.

## 3. Results (real robot success)

| Model | Seen tasks | Unseen instructions | Distractors | Backgrounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gato | 65% | 52% | 43% | 35% |
| BC-Z | 72% | 19% | 47% | 41% |
| **RT-1** | **97%** | **76%** | **83%** | **59%** |

- **Long-horizon with SayCan:** 67% execution in two real kitchens, against 13–53% for BC-Z.
- **Absorbs sim data:** adding sim demos kept real performance (92% to 90%) and raised success on sim-only objects from 23% to 87%.
- **Absorbs another robot's data:** adding Kuka bin-picking data raised bin-picking from 22% to 39% with almost no loss elsewhere.
- **Diversity beats quantity:** removing 25% of the *tasks* hurt generalization more than removing a large share of the *episodes*.

## 4. Limitations
- It is imitation learning, so it can't beat the demonstrators and doesn't learn from its own failures.
- It only generalizes to *new combinations* of seen concepts, not to new motions.
- Tasks are not dexterous, and it is weaker on new backgrounds.
- The data budget (130k demos, 17 months, 13 robots) is far beyond a course project.

## 5. How it relates to your SO-101 project
- **Background, not a method to copy.** It is the canonical citation for "one policy, many tasks via task-agnostic training at scale", and the reason the field moved towards generalist policies.
- **Useful lesson: diversity matters more than quantity.** This directly supports a curriculum that *chooses which scenes and goals to train on* (your failure-guided curriculum) over simply adding more uniform data. Cite it in the motivation for A/B/C.
- **Sim data helps a real policy.** The sim-absorption result supports mixing MuJoCo-generated data with your few real trials.
- **The contrast that motivates your approach:** RT-1 needs about 130k real demos. You have about 150 real trials, which is why you train in sim with RL and use real data only to find failures.
