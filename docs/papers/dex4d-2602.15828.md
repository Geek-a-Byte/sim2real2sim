# Dex4D (arXiv 2602.15828v2): explained

**Title:** Dex4D: Task-Agnostic Point Track Policy for Zero-Shot Dexterous Manipulation
**Authors:** Yuxuan Kuang, Sungjae Park, Katerina Fragkiadaki, Shubham Tulsiani (CMU). v1 Feb 2026, v2 Oct 13, 2026
**Project page:** https://dex4d.github.io/ (no code link listed)

## 1. The problem
Dexterous hands need a lot of data, and it is hard to get. Teleoperation is slow, and building a separate sim environment and reward for every task does not scale. Dex4D trains **one task-agnostic skill** in sim instead: "move this rigid object from whatever pose it is in to whatever pose I show you." At test time a video-generation model supplies the task as a sequence of target poses.

**Core idea:** the task lives in the *goal* (object point tracks taken from a generated video). The policy only has to learn one general skill, which they call **Anypose-to-Anypose (AP2AP)**.

## 2. Method
**The AP2AP training task**
- Each episode starts from a random object pose. When the robot reaches a goal, the next goal is sampled close to the achieved pose (within 10 cm and 0.5 rad), so a single episode chains many goals.
- 3,200 objects from UniDexGrasp, in Isaac Gym.

**Goal encoding: Paired Point Encoding** (the key design choice)
- Each object point is written as **[current xyz, target xyz]**, a 6-D "paired point". These go through a PointNet-style encoder (shared MLP, then mean+max pooling).
- This keeps the link between each point and its target, and the order of the points doesn't matter. Encoding current and target clouds separately did much worse (see the ablations).

**Teacher-student training**
- **Teacher:** PPO with privileged state (fingertip forces, joint torques, a 64-D object feature, 128 object points). The reward combines point goal distance, finger/hand-object closeness, a success bonus, and penalties for finger curl, table collisions, and action size.
- **Curriculum:** three stages, from bottles only to all objects, with reward weights changing per stage and control slowing from 30 Hz to 5 Hz in stage 3.
- **Student:** DAgger distillation to what a real camera can see: proprioception, the last action, and **64 masked, noisy paired points**. It is a small transformer (4 layers, dim 128) trained with an L1 BC loss plus an auxiliary loss for predicting the next joint state ("dynamics modeling").
- **Masking to mimic a single-view camera:** remove points behind a random plane, remove 90% of the points above a random height, and add 5 mm noise.
- Domain randomization covers mass, friction, stiffness and damping, gravity, and noise, plus random pushes on the object every 4 s.
- Compute: one RTX A6000, about 2–3 days for the teacher and about 20 h for the student. 4,096 envs.

**Deployment (zero-shot, no real fine-tuning)**
1. Wan2.6 generates a 5 s video of the task from the first frame plus a text prompt.
2. SAM2 segments the object, CoTracker3 tracks points through the video, and Video Depth Anything lifts them to 3-D (calibrated to the real first depth frame).
3. Closed loop: online CoTracker3 and RGB-D back-projection give the current points. The robot moves to the next target frame once the mean point error is below a threshold (the value isn't given).

**Hardware:** xArm6 with a LEAP hand (22 DoF), one RealSense D435, AprilTag calibration.

## 3. Results
**Sim, zero-shot, 6 tasks, 100 trials each**

| Method | Closed-loop | Success | Task progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| LVP (video, then hand retargeting) | no | 14.3% | 37.3% |
| NovaFlow (grasp, then Kabsch pose transfer) | no | 34.5% | 44.8% |
| NovaFlow-CL | yes | 43.7% | 60.8% |
| **Dex4D** | yes | **60.0%** | **71.2%** |

Per task, Apple2Plate and Pour reach 86%, but Hammer is only 28% and RotateBox 44%.

**Real, zero-shot, 4 tasks, 20 trials each**

| Method | LiftToy | Broccoli2Plate | Meat2Bowl | Pour | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LVP | 9 | 6 | 7 | 0 | 22/80 |
| NovaFlow-CL | 10 | 7 | 7 | 0 | 24/80 |
| π0.5-DROID (parallel gripper) | 5 | 6 | 9 | 0 | 20/80 |
| **Dex4D** | **14** | **10** | **13** | **8** | **45/80 (56%)** |

That is about 1.9× the best baseline. The 95% CI on the overall rate is [45%, 67%]. They also show qualitative generalization to new objects, backgrounds, camera views, trajectories, and pushes.

**Ablations (student, sim)**

| Variant | Success |
|---|---|
| Plain MLP on points | 5.7% |
| Separate current/target encodings | 20.3% |
| No self-attention | 49.0% |
| No dynamics-prediction loss | 57.0% |
| **Full** | **60.0%** |

Paired encoding is by far the biggest factor. Dropping from 64 to 32 points hurts only slightly, and the policy still works with fewer than 10.

**Where it fails:** video hallucinations (the only planning failure they saw), grasps that knock the object over, and the online tracker losing the object during fast motion or near similar textures.

## 4. Limitations
- Only single rigid objects. Articulated and deformable objects are left for later.
- **The policy sees only the manipulated object.** It ignores the target container and any obstacles, so it is not scene-aware.
- There is no replanning. If the video plan is wrong, nothing fixes it, although regenerating the video from the current frame would fit the same interface.
- It does not learn from its own failures. It is pure zero-shot, and real failures are only analysed by hand.
- It needs heavy sim compute and a dexterous hand. No human grasp priors are used.
- 20 real trials per task is small, so the CIs are wide.

## 5. How it relates to your SO-101 project
This is the closest match yet to your "one goal-conditioned policy" pitch. It is the SimToolReal idea (one policy for any goal pose of any object, with the task given as goals) applied to a dexterous hand, with goals coming from generated videos instead of hand-written ones.

**What transfers directly**
- **AP2AP as the training task.** Sample random start and goal poses and chain the goals inside an episode. This is a task-agnostic training objective that fits the professor's "one problem, one method" feedback. Tube-to-rack, pushing, and puzzle tasks are all just different goal sequences.
- **Paired Point Encoding for the goal.** It's cheap, and it's the most important ablation in the paper (20% to 60%). Use it for the goal input of your PPO policy, with a small number of points (they show 32 or fewer is fine, which suits an SO-101 webcam).
- **Teacher-student with point masking** is a clean recipe for a single webcam. Train a privileged PPO teacher in MuJoCo, then DAgger it into a student that sees masked, noisy points. You already run 4,096 MuJoCo envs for T3.
- **The goal pipeline matches your human-demo direction.** SAM2 and CoTracker3 on a *human demo video* (instead of a generated one) give you object point tracks as goals. This is the same tooling that OmniTacTune uses for its reward.

**Where you can go beyond it (your contributions)**
- **Scene-aware goals.** Their policy is blind to obstacles and the target. Adding scene points (the rack, the puzzle walls) as extra tokens fixes the limitation they name themselves.
- **Failure-guided curriculum.** Dex4D never learns from real failures. Your Fail2Progress-style loop (real failure, rebuild it in MuJoCo, oversample nearby start/goal pairs) addresses exactly the failure modes they list: knocked-over objects and hard rotations like Hammer at 28%.

**What doesn't transfer / watch out**
- **Gripper vs. hand.** SO-101 has 5 DoF and a parallel gripper, so the grasp is much simpler but in-hand rotation is impossible. Limit AP2AP goals to poses that are reachable for SO-101, or the policy will be trained on goals it can never reach.
- **Compute.** 2–3 days on an A6000 for the teacher with 3,200 objects. Use a handful of task objects (tube, puck, puzzle pieces) instead.
- **Tracking on a low-cost webcam.** They use RGB-D for 3-D points. With one RGB webcam you'll need known object models or pose estimation to get 3-D points, so plan for that.

Cite it as the main precedent for "one task-agnostic, goal-conditioned sim policy with object-centric goals, zero-shot to real". Then frame your contributions as scene awareness plus a failure-guided curriculum on top of it.
