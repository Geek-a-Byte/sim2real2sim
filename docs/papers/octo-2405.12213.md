# Octo (arXiv 2405.12213): explained

**Title:** Octo: An Open-Source Generalist Robot Policy
**Authors:** Octo Model Team (Ghosh, Walke, Pertsch, Black, Mees, … Sadigh, Finn, Levine), RSS 2024 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12213) · [project / code](https://octo-models.github.io)

## 1. The problem
Generalist policies such as RT-1/RT-2 were closed and tied to one robot. The authors want an **open** pretrained policy that anyone can **finetune to their own robot**, even one with new cameras or a new action space, on a consumer GPU.

## 2. Method
- **Data:** 800k trajectories from 25 Open X-Embodiment datasets (many robots).
- **Model:** a transformer over tokens. Language goes through a frozen T5-base (16 tokens). Images and goal images go through a shallow CNN and 16×16 patches (256 tokens for the third-person camera, 64 for the wrist camera). Learned **readout tokens** feed a small **diffusion action head** that predicts **action chunks**. The model comes in Small (27M) and Base (93M).
- **Task conditioning:** a language instruction **or a goal image**.
- **Finetuning:** about 100 demos, 50k steps, under 5 h on one 24 GB GPU. You can add new observation inputs or a new action head.

## 3. Results
- **Zero-shot:** 29% higher average success than RT-1-X, and roughly as good as RT-2-X (55B params). On WidowX, goal images beat language by 25%.
- **Finetuning on 6 new setups** (about 100 demos each):

| Method | Avg success |
|---|---|
| Train from scratch (ResNet+Transformer) | 20% |
| VC-1 pretrained visual features | 15% |
| **Octo finetuned** | **72%** |

- **Ablations:** diffusion head 83% vs. MSE head 35% vs. discretized actions 18%. The wide data mix (83%) beats single-robot Bridge data (43%). Action chunking helped, and **proprioceptive inputs hurt**.

## 4. Limitations
- It handles wrist-camera inputs poorly.
- Language conditioning is weaker than goal-image conditioning, because the data has limited annotations.
- It is pure imitation of demos, with no RL and no learning from failures.
- No mobile manipulation or navigation.

## 5. How it relates to your SO-101 project
- **This is the "finetune a pretrained generalist" alternative to your sim-RL approach.** LeRobot users finetune similar policies (Octo, SmolVLA, π0) on SO-101. It's a fair **baseline** to mention: about 100 teleop demos per task, finetuned. Your argument is that a sim-trained goal-conditioned RL policy plus a failure-guided curriculum needs fewer real demos and *improves from failures*, which imitation-based Octo can't do.
- **Goal-image conditioning beat language.** This supports conditioning on an *object-centric goal* (pose or points) instead of on text. That is consistent with Dex4D and Track2Act.
- **Design tips worth copying into your PPO student or BC distillation:** action chunking and a diffusion or expressive action head beat MSE. Also be careful with raw proprioception, which caused overfitting for them.
- **Practical caveat:** Octo-sized finetuning (under 5 h on a 24 GB GPU) is feasible, but the SO-101 isn't in OXE, so expect it to behave like a new embodiment.
