# Active Task Randomization (arXiv 2211.06134): explained

**Title:** Active Task Randomization: Learning Robust Skills via Unsupervised Generation of Diverse and Feasible Tasks
**Authors:** Kuan Fang, Toki Migimatsu, Ajay Mandlekar, Li Fei-Fei, Jeannette Bohg (Stanford / NVIDIA), IROS 2023 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.06134) · [project](https://sites.google.com/view/active-task-randomization/)

## 1. The problem
Robust skills need training tasks (scene layouts plus goals) that cover what the robot will meet at test time. Designing these by hand takes a lot of labor, and uniform random generation produces many **infeasible or useless** tasks. **ATR generates the training tasks automatically and picks the ones that are both diverse and feasible**, forming a curriculum.

## 2. Method
- **A task is a parameter vector w:** objects and their sizes, a **scene graph** of initial spatial relations, the skill and its target objects, and environment context (camera pose, point-cloud noise). A procedural generator builds w in PyBullet. Up to 6 objects, 54-D.
- **Feasibility:** a learned value V(w), which predicts the current policy's success on the task.
- **Diversity:** how rare w is among tasks already trained on (negative log density, using a k-nearest-neighbour estimate over the replay buffer).
- **Selection:** score = V + 0.1 · diversity. Tasks are sampled with a softmax over scores, plus 10% uniform sampling. The result is moderate difficulty and growing variety, so the curriculum becomes more complex as skills improve.
- **Task embedding:** a relational (graph) network over the scene graph, trained jointly with the value function.
- **Skills:** place-onto, place-next-to, push-under, and pull-with (using a tool). Each skill is a policy from segmented point clouds to end-effector waypoints, executed with an operational-space controller. Training is self-supervised on binary success. A **PDDL planner** chains skills, with replanning.
- **Hardware:** Franka Panda with a Kinect2.

## 3. Results
- **Single skills:** ATR matched or beat Uniform, VDS (value-disagreement sampling), and PLR (prioritized level replay) on all 4 skills, with the biggest gains on place-onto and pull-with. Exact numbers are only in a figure.
- **Sequential tasks:** **88.6–95.8% in sim**, while baselines often couldn't finish within 10 steps. On the **real robot it reached 40–100%** (10 trials per task).
- Ablations: feasibility-only and diversity-only were both worse than the combination.

## 4. Limitations
- The skill set is predefined.
- The task planner is hand-written and needs an external segmentation pipeline.
- Scene-graph relations come from hand-crafted heuristics.
- Real evaluation is small (10 trials per task).

## 5. How it relates to your SO-101 project
**This is the most important paper in the list for your *curriculum* half. It's the closest prior work to "which scenes and goals should we train on?"**
- **It's your baseline (B) done better.** ATR picks training tasks from the policy's *own sim* value estimates, which is a learned, sim-only curriculum. Your claim is that **real failures (C)** find gaps that sim-only signals like ATR's miss, because of the sim-to-real gap. Add ATR (or the simpler PLR) as a strong sim-curriculum comparison next to Uniform (A). That makes the A/B/C experiment much harder to dismiss.
- **Reusable mechanics:**
  - A **parameterized task vector with a scene graph** is a clean way to define your "scene-aware goals" (rack slots, puzzle walls, and gates as graph nodes and relations).
  - The **feasibility + diversity score** is a ready formula. A natural extension is **feasibility + diversity + closeness to real failures**: oversample tasks near the embeddings of real failures. That is a precise, novel statement of your curriculum.
  - The **10% uniform sampling** guards against the curriculum collapsing. Keep it.
- **How it differs from yours:** ATR trains separate skills and chains them with a planner. You plan one goal-conditioned policy. ATR also never uses real-world data in its curriculum, and that's your gap to fill.
